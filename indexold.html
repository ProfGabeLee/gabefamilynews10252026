This Week's Reflection

I wanted to share something with you about the Mental Health First Aid (MHFA) training I attended last Friday.

After going through my own struggles with mental health, particularly insomnia and anxiety, my perspective on mental health challenges has completely changed. In the past, I honestly thought mental health problems were something that happened to other people—people who were mentally weak, lazy, lacked willpower, or simply made excuses. I never imagined that I would experience something like this myself. But I learned the hard way that mental health challenges can happen to anyone, including me.

One of the biggest lessons I learned from my own experience was just how difficult, and sometimes almost impossible, it can be to overcome these struggles through willpower alone. I desperately wanted to feel better, think positively, and live my normal life. But I felt trapped in overwhelming darkness and physical exhaustion. Even when I wanted to do something positive, I simply couldn't find the strength to do it. It was a frightening and humbling experience. Thankfully, I've been able to recover to some extent, although I know I'm still vulnerable.

For me, God and the Bible have been the most powerful sources of healing, strength, and comfort. I'm deeply grateful that I have faith in God and can lean on Him during difficult times. But I also realize that many people, including devoted Christians struggle with similar challenges. Faith doesn't necessarily make us immune to emotional suffering.

That's one of the reasons I decided to attend the MHFA training. I wanted to learn how to better cope with mental health challenges if they return, but I also wanted to understand how I could support others who are going through similar struggles.

To be honest, the training wasn't quite as helpful or engaging as I had hoped. Still, I did take away some useful lessons about how to approach, listen to, and support people who are struggling, especially my students who may be facing mental health challenges while trying to manage their academic and personal lives.

Looking back, I sometimes wonder whether God allowed me to go through this difficult season so that I could better understand and care for others who are suffering. I don't know the answer, but I'm praying that God will use my experience for something meaningful. I hope that, by sharing my story, I can help someone feel a little less alone, a little more understood, and perhaps a little more hopeful. Sometimes, just knowing that someone else has walked through a similar darkness and understands your pain can bring tremendous comfort.

And finally, here's one thing I want to share with all of you. I hope you continue to grow closer to God and develop a personal relationship with Him. To me, He is not just someone we turn to on Sundays or when life gets difficult. He is our closest friend, our protector, our source of strength, and our healer.

Life will inevitably bring challenges that we cannot always overcome on our own. But I hope you will always remember that you can turn to God, lean on Him, and find peace and strength in His presence.

With love and prayers,

Daddy ❤️
