This Week's Reflection

Hey all, Quite a few things happened this week, and I wanted to share two experiences with you.

The most unexpected and unforgettable experience was an active threat incident ('gunshot alarm') on campus. In the middle of my class, campus-wide emergency alarms suddenly went off, warning us of a possible shooting nearby. At first, I was confused and didn't know exactly what to do. But almost immediately, my first thought was that I needed to protect my students. So I quickly locked the classroom door, turned off the lights, and tried to help everyone stay calm.

There was one particularly frightening moment when someone tried to open our classroom door. I still don't know who it was or why they were trying to get in, but that moment is something I don't think I'll ever forget. After about 20 minutes, we finally received the all-clear following the police investigation.

First of all, I was incredibly grateful to God that nothing terrible happened and nobody was injured. But looking back, I was also a little surprised by how calm I remained during such a frightening and uncertain situation. I think my faith in God had a lot to do with it. Because I believe in Him and trust in His promise of eternal life, even the thought of death doesn't frighten me as much as it once might have. But I also believe that my life is ultimately in God's hands, and that gives me a deep sense of peace, even in moments of fear.

Life is full of unexpected events, and we never know what tomorrow may bring. But as long as we stay close to God and place our trust in Him, we can find peace and strength no matter what happens. That was the biggest lesson I took away from this experience, and it's something I wanted to share with you.

On a much happier note, I also learned this week that I was once again named among the World's Top 2% Scientists published by Stanford University and Elsevier! Every year, they release a list recognizing researchers whose scholarly work has made a significant impact in their respective fields. This isn't my first time being included, but it still brings me a wonderful feeling of gratitude and accomplishment. You know, I have spent countless hours sitting alone in my little office, reading, thinking, writing, revising, and sometimes struggling to get my research published. Academic research can be a long, lonely, and frustrating journey. So it is especially meaningful to know that those countless hours of hard work have not been wasted—that other researchers around the world are reading my work, citing it, and finding value in the ideas I have contributed to our field. It makes me feel that, in my own small way, I have contributed something meaningful to academic knowledge and perhaps even to society. But more than anything, I thank God for giving me the talents, opportunities, patience, and perseverance to pursue this challenging academic career.

As I reflect on these two very different experiences this week, one frightening and the other joyful, I realize that both remind me of how blessed I am and how grateful I should be to God. One reminded me how fragile and unpredictable life can be, while the other reminded me how meaningful our efforts can become when we faithfully use the gifts God has given us.

Love you guys always! ❤️
